# Open Research Questions Synthesis — Distilled View of 262 ORQs

This section distills the 262 open research questions (ORQs) consolidated in [`catalog/13-open-research-questions.md`](../catalog/13-open-research-questions.md) into a prioritised, thematic synthesis. The ORQ list is the design team's pre-implementation backlog: every question must be answered (or explicitly deferred) before the corresponding capability can be implemented. The catalog's 130 problems identify what's hard; the 262 ORQs identify what's undecided. This synthesis does not restate every ORQ — it surfaces the 11 Tier-1 architectural questions that gate design, the 12 cross-cutting themes that recur across capabilities, and the research spikes the team should run before design begins.

## 1. ORQ landscape

The catalog consolidates **262 open research questions across 12 capabilities**, averaging ~2.0 ORQs per source problem. Every "Open questions" entry from the 130-problem catalog has been extracted, numbered (ORQ-001 through ORQ-262), grouped by capability, and tagged with its source problem (PC-NNN). The questions are not solutions — they are decisions to be made, trade-offs to be evaluated, and experiments to be run. Some are binary (adopt X vs Y); others are open-ended (what is the cost of Z?).

The ORQs are tiered by when they must be answered in the framework's design lifecycle. **Tier 1** questions are architectural — they cascade across multiple capabilities and must be answered before design begins. Picking wrong is expensive to undo. **Tier 2** questions are per-capability design decisions that should be locked in before implementation begins. **Tier 3** questions are per-feature decisions that can be made incrementally as code is written. The catalog identifies **11 Tier-1 questions**, ~30 Tier-2 questions, and ~220 Tier-3 questions. The remainder of this synthesis focuses on Tier 1 (because that's where the architectural leverage is), summarises Tier 2 by capability, notes that Tier 3 is implementation-deferred, and proposes concrete research spikes. See [`catalog/13-open-research-questions.md`](../catalog/13-open-research-questions.md) for the full enumeration.

## 2. The 11 Tier-1 architectural questions

These 11 questions gate the design phase. Each one cascades across multiple capabilities, and the wrong answer forces rework. The expected duration of a research spike per Tier-1 question is 1–2 weeks; the team should plan to run all 11 in parallel during the architecture phase.

**ORQ-001 / ORQ-002 / ORQ-003 / ORQ-004 — Replication protocol choice (DRSUAPI vs. CRDT vs. Raft).** *Why architectural:* Replication is the foundation of Core Directory; every Operations, Migration, and Security problem depends on it. *Candidate answers:* (a) Implement DRSUAPI server-side (full AD wire-compat, forces UTD vector model, ~6 person-months); (b) CRDT/OT replication with a DRSUAPI wire-compat shim on top (preserves interop, adds complexity, unproven at AD scale); (c) Raft consensus, abandon AD-interop (clean-slate, strong consistency, breaks every AD-interop scenario). *Cascade:* if (a), the UTD vector and `PROPERTY_META_DATA_EXT` are forced; if (c), the migration story (PC-124, PC-126) must be re-designed from scratch. *Recommendation:* research spike comparing (a) and (b) on a 1M-object test corpus; (c) is only viable if AD-interop is explicitly out of scope.

**ORQ-011 / ORQ-012 / ORQ-013 / ORQ-014 — Storage engine choice (ESE vs. SQLite vs. FoundationDB vs. custom).** *Why architectural:* The storage engine determines throughput, transactional semantics, backup/restore model, and replication log structure. *Candidate answers:* (a) SQLite WAL (mature, embedded, single-node — limits DC throughput to ~5K writes/sec); (b) FoundationDB (distributed, ordered KV, scales horizontally — adds an operational dependency); (c) RocksDB (LSM-tree, embedded, scales to 10M objects — operational tuning required); (d) Custom (full control, multi-year investment). *Cascade:* the engine choice determines backup/restore (PC-020), tombstone lifetime (PC-009), and sdtable design (PC-008). *Recommendation:* spike FoundationDB and RocksDB; SQLite is too slow for production DCs; custom is unjustified.

**ORQ-026 / ORQ-027 — Identity model (SIDs vs. UUIDs vs. both).** *Why architectural:* SIDs are the interop currency with AD; UUIDs are the modern equivalent; using both requires a mapping layer that touches every capability. *Candidate answers:* (a) SIDs only (full AD interop, forecloses future modernisation); (b) UUIDs only (modern, breaks AD interop entirely); (c) Both with mapping (interop + modern, mapping complexity in every ACL check). *Cascade:* PC-015 (RID pool), PC-089 (ID mapping on POSIX), PC-124 (sIDHistory migration), PC-127 (password hash migration). *Recommendation:* both with mapping — SIDs as the wire format for AD interop, UUIDs as the internal primary key, with a cached bidirectional mapping table. The mapping table is itself a Tier-2 design problem (ORQ-177/178).

**ORQ-030 / ORQ-031 — Schema model (LDAP dynamic vs. typed).** *Why architectural:* Schema choice determines the directory API surface, the replication payload format, and the client SDK type system. *Candidate answers:* (a) LDAP dynamic schema (full AD compat, runtime-extensible, no compile-time type safety); (b) Typed schema (protobuf/SQL DDL, compile-time safety, no runtime extension); (c) Hybrid — LDAP schema with typed projection (best of both, adapter cost). *Cascade:* PC-017 (schema format), PC-021 (bitmask vs. explicit columns), PC-046 (ADMX-equivalent), PC-058 (cert template schema), PC-107 (schema-as-code), PC-113 (functional levels). *Recommendation:* hybrid — LDAP schema as the wire format, typed projection as the SDK surface, with code generation from the LDAP schema definition.

**ORQ-042 / ORQ-043 / ORQ-044 — KDC implementation (Samba Heimdal vs. MIT+plugin vs. fresh).** *Why architectural:* The KDC is the second-most-critical capability; PAC generation and signing must match MS-KILE byte-for-byte or AD-aware services reject tickets. *Candidate answers:* (a) Samba Heimdal fork (GPLv3, only open-source server that emits MS-PAC, license risk for commercial framework); (b) MIT krb5 + custom PAC plugin (FreeIPA approach, MIT-licensed, requires re-implementing PAC generation); (c) Fresh implementation (full control, multi-year effort, high defect risk). *Cascade:* PC-023 (MS-KILE profile), PC-024 (RC4 default), PC-030 (krbtgt rotation), PC-035 (gMSA), every Kerberos-related Security problem. *Recommendation:* spike MIT krb5 + custom PAC plugin against the Samba Heimdal implementation; license-permissive path is required for most framework adoption.

**ORQ-072 / ORQ-074 / ORQ-075 — NTLM decision (drop vs. maintain compat vs. maintain with hardening).** *Why architectural:* NTLM is a security liability but is required by ~5–10% of enterprise apps. *Candidate answers:* (a) Drop NTLM entirely (eliminates pass-the-hash and relay attack classes, breaks legacy apps); (b) Maintain NTLM with hard mitigations (channel binding, EPA, signing, LSASS protection — preserves compat, complexity); (c) Maintain NTLM via Samba winbind on a separate daemon (preserves compat, isolates risk). *Cascade:* PC-036, PC-037, PC-038, PC-039 (Auth Provider), PC-094 (macOS parity), every Client SDK auth path. *Recommendation:* maintain NTLM via Samba winbind (option c) for v1, with a documented deprecation timeline; the hardening mitigations (option b) are required regardless.

**ORQ-110 / ORQ-111 — PKI protocol (MS-WCCE vs. ACME vs. EST).** *Why architectural:* PKI enrollment protocol determines whether Windows clients can auto-enroll and whether the framework can interop with AD CS hierarchies. *Candidate answers:* (a) Implement MS-WCCE/MS-XCEP (full AD interop, complex, no open-source implementation); (b) Adopt ACME (RFC 8555, modern, simple, requires Windows-side adapter for auto-enroll); (c) Adopt EST (RFC 7030, simpler than MS-WCCE, less ecosystem support than ACME); (d) Single enrollment endpoint speaking all three (most compat, most engineering). *Cascade:* PC-057 (AD CS replacement), PC-059 (autoenrollment), PC-064 (NDES), PC-123 (supply chain). *Recommendation:* ACME as primary, with a Windows-side adapter that translates autoenroll-equivalent GPO to ACME account+order flows.

**ORQ-132 / ORQ-133 / ORQ-134 — Federation layer (re-implement AD FS vs. wrap modern IdP vs. cloud-first).** *Why architectural:* Federation is the framework's web/HTTP identity surface; the choice determines operational weight, IdP feature set, and migration path. *Candidate answers:* (a) Re-implement AD FS (full wire-compat for legacy relying parties, multi-year effort, fragile); (b) Wrap Keycloak (Apache-licensed, mature, lighter than AD FS, requires SAML/OIDC config translation); (c) Wrap Authentik / Ory / Zitadel (cloud-native, smaller ecosystem); (d) Cloud-first (recommend Entra ID for orgs that want cloud). *Cascade:* PC-068 through PC-077 (every Federation problem). *Recommendation:* wrap Keycloak for v1; AD FS wire-compat (option a) only if a paying customer requires it.

**ORQ-154 / ORQ-155 — SMB server (Samba vs. fresh vs. platform-native).** *Why architectural:* SMB is the most dangerous protocol surface (3 blockers in File Gateway); the server choice determines security posture and operational model. *Candidate answers:* (a) Adopt Samba's smbd (GPLv3, mature, license risk); (b) Fresh SMB server implementation (full control, multi-year, high defect risk on SMB 3.1.1 pre-auth integrity); (c) Reuse macOS SMBX kernel ext (macOS-only, doesn't help Linux/Windows); (d) Wrap platform-native (Samba on Linux, SMBX on macOS, srv2.sys on Windows — but Windows can't host framework DCs if the framework's DC runs Linux). *Cascade:* PC-078 (SMB 3.1.1), PC-079 (SMB1 drop), PC-083 (PrintNightmare), every Client SDK file client. *Recommendation:* Samba for v1 (license risk accepted or negotiated), with a fresh-implementation research track for v2.

**ORQ-169 / ORQ-170 / ORQ-175 / ORQ-176 — Client SDK architecture (unified C/Rust/Go vs. per-platform wrappers).** *Why architectural:* The SDK is the developer-facing surface; the choice determines API consistency, language coverage, and maintenance cost. *Candidate answers:* (a) gRPC-based SDK with platform-native auth adapters (consistent API across languages, gRPC adds a runtime dependency); (b) Rust core with per-language bindings (C FFI, no gRPC dependency, more binding work); (c) Per-platform wrappers around existing libraries (SSSD on Linux, OpenDirectory on macOS, Wldap32 on Windows — fastest to ship, inherits each platform's quirks); (d) Adopt FreeIPA client as the base (Linux only, doesn't help macOS/Windows). *Cascade:* PC-085 (no universal SDK), PC-089 (ID mapping), PC-090 (Heimdal vs MIT), PC-091 (domain join), PC-093 (ticket cache), every parity problem. *Recommendation:* Rust core with C FFI bindings and per-language wrappers (option b); gRPC (option a) is too heavy for the client side.

**ORQ-202 / ORQ-203 — Linux tier strategy (SSSD only vs. SSSD+Winbind vs. FreeIPA).** *Why architectural:* Linux identity is fragmented across three stacks; the framework must pick one or build a fourth. *Candidate answers:* (a) SSSD only (modern default, no NTLM, no full GPO CSE — see PC-088); (b) SSSD + Winbind (SSSD for NSS/PAM, Winbind for SMB/NTLM — operational complexity); (c) FreeIPA (full-featured, separate platform, AD cross-forest trust); (d) Build native IPA-equivalent in the framework (multi-year, full control). *Cascade:* PC-088 (SSSD gaps), PC-099 (stack migration), PC-101 (FreeIPA), PC-103 (OpenLDAP+MIT). *Recommendation:* adopt FreeIPA as the Linux tier for v1 (option c), with a documented migration path from SSSD-only deployments; building native (option d) is v2+ work.

## 3. The 12 cross-cutting themes

The catalog identifies 12 cross-cutting themes — research questions that recur across multiple capabilities and cannot be answered by a single capability team in isolation. The first 10 mirror the design tensions synthesised in [`./03-problem-catalog-synthesis.md`](./03-problem-catalog-synthesis.md) §7; the last two (HSM binding, GitOps) are unique to the ORQ list.

**AD-interop vs. clean-slate (the meta-question).** This is the foundational tension that subsumes every other cross-cutting theme. Spans 13 problems: PC-001, PC-002, PC-007, PC-017, PC-023, PC-036, PC-057, PC-068, PC-078, PC-094, PC-099, PC-103, PC-124. The framework must pick a lane per protocol — full compat, compat-with-shim, or clean-slate — and the answer is different for each protocol. Replication (DRSUAPI) is the highest-stakes instantiation; the rest follow from it. Related ORQs: ORQ-001, ORQ-002, ORQ-030, ORQ-031, ORQ-042, ORQ-043, ORQ-044, ORQ-072, ORQ-110, ORQ-132, ORQ-133, ORQ-154, ORQ-155, ORQ-202, ORQ-203, ORQ-249, ORQ-250.

**Multi-master vs. consensus.** AD is multi-master with last-writer-wins; modern systems prefer Raft/Paxos. Spans 8 problems: PC-002, PC-009, PC-014, PC-016, PC-022, PC-043, PC-074, PC-108. The framework must decide per-NC (naming context) whether to use multi-master (compat) or consensus (correctness). The Raft answer simplifies correctness but breaks AD-interop; the multi-master answer preserves interop but inherits LWW semantics. Related ORQs: ORQ-003, ORQ-004, ORQ-016, ORQ-017, ORQ-024, ORQ-025, ORQ-083, ORQ-146, ORQ-147, ORQ-216, ORQ-217.

**LDAP vs. typed schema.** AD's schema is dynamic, LDAP-defined; modern systems prefer typed schemas. Spans 6 problems: PC-017, PC-021, PC-046, PC-058, PC-107, PC-113. The choice cascades into the directory API, replication protocol, and client SDK. The hybrid answer (LDAP wire format + typed projection) is the most promising. Related ORQs: ORQ-030, ORQ-031, ORQ-038, ORQ-039, ORQ-088, ORQ-089, ORQ-112, ORQ-113, ORQ-214, ORQ-215, ORQ-227, ORQ-228.

**SIDs vs. UUIDs.** AD uses SIDs; modern systems prefer UUIDs. Spans 5 problems: PC-015, PC-026, PC-089, PC-124, PC-127. The "both with mapping" answer is operationally expensive but preserves both interop and modernisation. sIDHistory migration (PC-124) is the immediate pressure point. Related ORQs: ORQ-026, ORQ-027, ORQ-177, ORQ-178, ORQ-241, ORQ-249, ORQ-250.

**GPO vs. declarative policy.** AD's GPO is INI/registry.pol-based, fragile, no rollback; modern alternatives are declarative, versioned, transactional. Spans 16 problems: PC-043 through PC-056, PC-095, PC-125, PC-130. The framework must decide per-policy-type whether to keep GPO format (interop), adopt declarative (modern), or hybrid (declarative source that compiles to GPO for Windows). Related ORQs: ORQ-082, ORQ-083, ORQ-088, ORQ-089, ORQ-090, ORQ-091, ORQ-100, ORQ-101, ORQ-189, ORQ-190, ORQ-191, ORQ-251, ORQ-252, ORQ-261, ORQ-262.

**NTLM drop vs. maintain.** NTLM is broken but legacy apps require it. Spans 5 problems: PC-036, PC-037, PC-038, PC-039, PC-094. The "maintain with hardening" answer is the realistic v1; the "drop entirely" answer is v2+. Related ORQs: ORQ-068, ORQ-069, ORQ-070, ORQ-071, ORQ-072, ORQ-074, ORQ-075, ORQ-187, ORQ-188.

**AD CS vs. ACME.** AD CS uses MS-WCCE/MS-XCEP; modern PKI uses ACME/EST. Spans 12 problems: PC-057 through PC-067, PC-123. The ACME answer is simpler and modern but requires a Windows-side adapter. Related ORQs: ORQ-110, ORQ-111, ORQ-124, ORQ-125, ORQ-126, ORQ-127, ORQ-128, ORQ-129, ORQ-130, ORQ-131, ORQ-247, ORQ-248.

**AD FS vs. modern IdP.** AD FS is heavy and Windows-bound; modern IdPs are lighter and cloud-native. Spans 10 problems: PC-068 through PC-077. The wrap-modern-IdP answer is obvious for new deployments; the re-implement answer is required only for orgs needing AD FS wire-compat. Related ORQs: ORQ-132, ORQ-133, ORQ-134, ORQ-135, ORQ-136, ORQ-137, ORQ-140, ORQ-141, ORQ-148, ORQ-149, ORQ-150, ORQ-151.

**Multi-tenancy.** AD has no native multi-tenancy; cloud-native systems expect it. Spans 5 problems: PC-022, PC-067, PC-076, PC-101, PC-102. The native answer adds complexity; the per-instance answer doesn't match cloud-native expectations. Related ORQs: ORQ-040, ORQ-041, ORQ-130, ORQ-131, ORQ-151, ORQ-202, ORQ-203, ORQ-204, ORQ-205.

**Client SDK unification.** No universal AD client SDK exists. Spans 19 problems: PC-085 through PC-093, PC-094 through PC-105. The unified SDK answer is more work but produces consistency; the per-platform answer is faster but inherits quirks. Related ORQs: ORQ-169, ORQ-170, ORQ-175, ORQ-176, ORQ-177, ORQ-178, ORQ-179, ORQ-180, ORQ-181, ORQ-182, ORQ-200, ORQ-201.

**HSM binding for high-value keys.** Several capabilities have keys that should never leave an HSM: krbtgt, KDS root key, KRA private keys, CA private keys, framework binary signing keys. Spans 6 problems: PC-035, PC-060, PC-066, PC-117, PC-118, PC-123. The framework must decide whether HSM binding is a hard requirement (Tier 0 feature) or optional (Tier 3 nice-to-have). The right answer is hard requirement for krbtgt, CA private keys, and framework signing keys; optional for KRA and KDS root. Related ORQs: ORQ-057, ORQ-063, ORQ-066, ORQ-067, ORQ-116, ORQ-117, ORQ-128, ORQ-236, ORQ-237, ORQ-247.

**GitOps for directory config.** Multiple capabilities hint at Git-backed declarative configuration: schema (PC-107), GPO (PC-043, PC-056), framework policies (PC-125). Spans 5 problems. The framework must decide whether to standardise on Git as the source of truth for all declarative configuration, with the directory as a materialised view. The GitOps answer gives version-controlled, audited, PR-reviewed config changes; the directory-native answer is simpler operationally. Related ORQs: ORQ-082, ORQ-106, ORQ-107, ORQ-108, ORQ-214, ORQ-215, ORQ-251, ORQ-252.

## 4. Tier-2 questions summary

Tier-2 questions are per-capability design decisions that should be locked in before implementation begins. The catalog identifies ~30 such questions across the 12 capabilities. Grouped by capability:

**Core Directory** (ORQ-024, ORQ-025, ORQ-032). FSMO replacement strategy (Raft-based consensus vs. multi-master with version vectors), and `memberOf` computation strategy (event-driven cache on write vs. read-time computation). The FSMO answer depends on the Tier-1 replication choice; the `memberOf` answer depends on read/write ratio and group size distribution.

**KDC** (ORQ-057, ORQ-058, ORQ-059, ORQ-060). krbtgt key management (HSM-bound vs. file-backed; auto-rotation interval), and SPN uniqueness scope (per-forest vs. per-domain). The krbtgt HSM answer is the golden-ticket mitigation; the SPN scope answer determines cross-domain conflict handling.

**Auth Provider** (no Tier-2 ORQs identified in the catalog — all Auth Provider questions are Tier 1 or Tier 3). The capability's design follows from the NTLM Tier-1 decision.

**Policy Engine** (ORQ-082, ORQ-083, ORQ-088, ORQ-089). Policy format (single declarative YAML per GPO vs. per-GPO CRDT vs. OPA Rego vs. custom DSL), and the HBAC-vs-URA access-control model (ORQ-102, ORQ-103). The policy format answer depends on the GitOps theme; the access-control answer depends on the Linux tier strategy.

**Cert Service** (ORQ-116, ORQ-117, ORQ-118, ORQ-122). KRA key management (HSM + Shamir secret sharing), and CRLite adoption for CRL compression. The CRLite answer is significant for large-forest PKI; Mozilla's deployment shows 1000× compression.

**Federation Gateway** (ORQ-135, ORQ-136, ORQ-137). Claims-policy language (Rego vs. Cedar vs. per-IdP plugins). The answer depends on the Tier-1 federation layer choice.

**File Gateway** (ORQ-161, ORQ-162). SMB container strategy (CSI + SMB-server-in-container vs. CTDB-style clustered Samba). The CSI answer is Kubernetes-native; the CTDB answer is Samba-native.

**Client SDK** (ORQ-177, ORQ-178, ORQ-194, ORQ-195). POSIX UID strategy (drop POSIX UIDs entirely vs. standardise on one algorithm), and disk-encryption key escrow (per-computer in directory vs. NBDE Clevis/Tang). Both answers depend on the Tier-1 Linux tier strategy.

**Cross-Platform Parity** (no Tier-2 ORQs identified; the capability is defined by the Tier-1 choices).

**Operations** (ORQ-214, ORQ-215, ORQ-218, ORQ-219, ORQ-224, ORQ-225, ORQ-226). Schema-as-code (Git-backed vs. typed-schema with versioned migrations), DC containerisation (per-DC image vs. operator), and the directory API surface (REST vs. gRPC vs. GraphQL). The API surface answer determines the operational CLI and SDK design.

**Security** (ORQ-233, ORQ-234, ORQ-237, ORQ-238, ORQ-241, ORQ-242, ORQ-247, ORQ-248). Kerberoasting detection (4769 etype 0x17 alerting + AES migration strategy), krbtgt HSM binding + auto-rotation, sIDHistory future (drop vs. per-trust filtering policy), and supply-chain (Sigstore + in-toto) adoption. The Kerberoasting and krbtgt answers follow from the Tier-1 KDC choice; the sIDHistory answer follows from the Tier-1 identity model choice.

**Migration** (ORQ-249, ORQ-250, ORQ-253, ORQ-254, ORQ-255, ORQ-256). sIDHistory migration alternative (claims-based vs. ADMT-only), migration granularity (per-SPN vs. per-user), and password-sync agent protocol (proprietary vs. standard). The migration answers follow from the Tier-1 replication and identity model choices.

## 5. Tier-3 questions summary

Tier-3 questions are per-feature decisions that can be made incrementally as code is written. The catalog identifies ~220 such questions across the 12 capabilities. They are deliberately deferred to implementation because they do not cascade and the cost of changing the answer later is bounded. Examples: ORQ-005 (linkID pairs vs. graph database for membership), ORQ-010 (schema cache lock elimination strategy), ORQ-035/036/037 (backup mechanism: CRIU vs. LVM vs. storage-engine-native), ORQ-055/056 (etype 0x13 AES SHA-384 adoption timing), ORQ-092/093 (per-CSE snapshot vs. Git-style revert), ORQ-138/139 (token-signing cert rollover notification), ORQ-157/158 (SMB1 hard cut vs. compat shim), ORQ-189/190/191 (policy DSL unification), ORQ-231/232 (unified CLI base: samba-tool vs. fresh Go/Rust). Engineers should pick the Tier-3 ORQ relevant to their current work, run a research spike, and document the decision in their PR description. The Tier-3 list is long precisely because the framework is large; the tiering ensures the design phase is not blocked on per-feature decisions.

## 6. Research spike recommendations

The team should run the following 7 research spikes before design begins. Each spike is sized to 1–3 engineers for 1–3 weeks, with a written deliverable.

**Spike 1 — DRSUAPI replication interop vs. CRDT-shim.** Investigate whether a CRDT/OT replication engine can be wrapped with a DRSUAPI wire-compat layer that preserves AD-interop semantics (UTD vector, `PROPERTY_META_DATA_EXT`, lingering-object detection). Build a 1M-object prototype with both pure-DRSUAPI and CRDT-shim backends; measure replication latency, throughput, and AD-interop correctness. Duration: 3 weeks. Deliverable: comparative benchmark report + recommendation for ORQ-001/002/003/004.

**Spike 2 — Storage engine evaluation: FoundationDB vs. RocksDB vs. SQLite.** Build a minimal directory store on each engine; measure LDAP read/write throughput at 1M, 10M, and 100M objects; measure backup/restore time; measure replication log throughput. Pay special attention to transactional semantics for multi-attribute updates within a single object. Duration: 3 weeks. Deliverable: benchmark report + recommendation for ORQ-011/012/013/014.

**Spike 3 — MIT krb5 + custom PAC plugin vs. Samba Heimdal fork.** Implement MS-PAC generation in a MIT krb5 KDB plugin (mirroring FreeIPA's `ipa_kdb_mspac.c`); validate byte-for-byte against AD-issued tickets. Test PAC acceptance by IIS, SQL Server, and Samba SMB server. Compare defect surface, license implications, and ongoing maintenance cost. Duration: 3 weeks. Deliverable: PAC byte-compat report + license analysis + recommendation for ORQ-042/043/044.

**Spike 4 — NTLM compat surface audit.** Catalog every NTLM-requiring app in 3–5 representative enterprise deployments (pre-2018 SQL Server drivers, legacy IIS apps, old SMB appliances). Quantify the NTLM-requiring workload and assess each app's Kerberos migration path. Determine whether Samba winbind as a sidecar (option c in ORQ-072/074/075) covers the realistic legacy surface. Duration: 2 weeks. Deliverable: NTLM compat matrix + recommendation for ORQ-072/074/075.

**Spike 5 — ACME + Windows autoenroll adapter.** Build a Windows-side adapter that translates AD-CS autoenroll-equivalent GPO to ACME account+order flows. Validate that Windows 10/11 clients can auto-enroll certificates from an ACME server (Step-CA or smallstep) without manual intervention. Compare to MS-WCCE implementation effort. Duration: 2 weeks. Deliverable: working adapter + recommendation for ORQ-110/111.

**Spike 6 — Keycloak as AD FS replacement.** Configure Keycloak to provide SAML 2.0, OIDC, and WS-Federation endpoints; translate an AD FS relying-party configuration (claims rules, RP trust) to Keycloak. Test with 3–5 representative SaaS apps (Salesforce, ServiceNow, Workday). Identify the WS-Trust-to-OIDC bridge requirement and the AD FS-specific quirks (`resource=` parameter, Application Groups). Duration: 2 weeks. Deliverable: AD FS-to-Keycloak migration playbook + recommendation for ORQ-132/133/134.

**Spike 7 — Rust core SDK with C FFI bindings.** Build a minimal Rust core that wraps LDAP bind, Kerberos kinit, and SMB mount; expose via C FFI; build Python and Go bindings. Measure API consistency vs. native SSSD/OpenDirectory/Wldap32 wrappers. Identify the platform-native auth adapters needed (Keychain on macOS, KCM on Linux, CredSSP on Windows). Duration: 3 weeks. Deliverable: working prototype + recommendation for ORQ-169/170/175/176.

These 7 spikes collectively cover 9 of the 11 Tier-1 architectural questions. The remaining 2 (ORQ-026/027 SID vs. UUID, ORQ-030/031 schema model) are architectural design decisions rather than empirical questions; they should be resolved in a 2-day architecture workshop with the spike results in hand. Total spike duration: 16 person-weeks; parallelised across 3–4 engineers, the architecture phase completes in 4–5 weeks. The team should not begin capability design until all 7 spikes are complete and the 11 Tier-1 questions are answered.

## Synthesis

The 262 ORQs resolve, in practice, to 11 architectural decisions (Tier 1), ~30 per-capability design decisions (Tier 2), and ~220 per-feature decisions (Tier 3). The 11 Tier-1 questions are not independent — they are per-protocol instantiations of the 10 cross-cutting tensions synthesised in [`./03-problem-catalog-synthesis.md`](./03-problem-catalog-synthesis.md) §7, plus the HSM binding and GitOps themes that are unique to the ORQ list. The 7 recommended research spikes cover 9 of the 11 Tier-1 questions; the remaining 2 are architectural design decisions resolved in a workshop. The architecture phase is 4–5 weeks of parallel spike work; capability design begins after.
